⚠️ Verdict: Compelling thesis (supply scarcity + ecosystem moat) but the 3.42x multiple must be substantiated with a residence sales model. EIA approval is a gate — consider structuring as a condition precedent.

Positives ✅

  • First-mover advantage: One of the last undeveloped premium atolls in the Maldives — genuine supply constraint
  • Infrastructure moat: Dedicated 2,200m runway + private terminal + hangar — extremely high barrier for competitors
  • World-class marine assets: 20+ dive sites, Fotheyo Kandu (ranked top global dive site), bioluminescent plankton — powerful differentiation
  • Architect pedigree: Snøhetta (Bibliotheca Alexandrina, Oslo Opera House) + Neri & Hu (Architizer A+ Firm of the Year 2023)
  • Proven execution: Aman Maldives late-stage construction on time and budget despite Middle East conflicts — lessons transferable (reclamation, residence pricing/sales)
  • 3-brand masterplan: Aman (luxury) + Janu (active/social) + ATMA (wellness) targets distinct guest profiles on the same land, maximizing land value per square meter

Critiques 🔴

  1. EIA pending for reclamation = single biggest risk in the entire portfolio: Without EIA approval, reclamation cannot proceed → no land to build on. Delay/denial pushes the entire 2030 timeline. This risk is outside investor control.
  2. 3.42x multiple has no profit breakdown: Is the $226.4M profit driven by residence sales or operations? If 60-70% from residences, velocity risk is the core risk (already self-flagged but unquantified).
  3. Cash call 2.6x initial ($67.4M future): Large future capital commitment requiring a multi-year liquidity plan.
  4. Long duration: Opening 2030 + stabilization → exit likely 2033-2035 (7-9 year hold). 24.1% IRR over this duration is reasonable only if residence sellout stays on schedule.
  5. Minority 49%: No control. Exit dependent on Aman (majority) alignment of interests.

  • ✅ Land and site already exist — no land acquisition risk

Positives ✅

  • Iconic brand DNA: Birthplace of the overwater bungalow concept — a brand story impossible to replicate
  • Strong US tourism base with spending power
  • Subsidies + tax incentives "in principle" — quantifiable return enhancement (needs NPV)
  • Scarce residential upside: Bora Bora residences at St. Regis/Four Seasons trade at extreme premiums with limited inventory
  • Pricing below valuation per the offering memo

Critiques 🔴

  1. IRR 20.1% still 2nd-lowest: Even as a lower-risk conversion, the spread vs Mykonos (34.4%) is 14pp. This needs explanation — if conservative assumptions, that's acceptable; if hidden conversion costs (demolition, remediation, heritage preservation), that's a concern.
  2. US tourism dependency ~50-60%: A US recession directly impacts Bora Bora disproportionately. Self-flagged but no sensitivity analysis provided.

  4. French Polynesia logistics still relevant: Even as a conversion, material/vessel logistics to a remote island are costly and slow — construction cost overrun risk remains (self-flagged).
  5. 2.27x multiple: Lowest among developments — confirm this is a fair risk-adjusted tradeoff (lower risk → lower return).
✅ Verdict: Best risk-balancing asset in the development cohort — conversion + approvals done makes this nearly operating-asset-like in risk profile. 2.27x/20% IRR is reasonable for this risk level. Needs: residence comps + subsidy NPV quantification.

  • Ultra-luxury positioning: Mykonos boasts the highest ADRs in Greece, few luxury hotel brands, significant pricing power
  • Strong residence demand: Ultra-high-end buyers are price-insensitive; limited Aman residence supply = scarcity premium
  • Design complete, ready for final permit amendments + construction start — near-term build-ready
  • Lowest future cash call ($19.0M) — least capital-intensive of the three developments

Critiques 🔴

  1. Seasonality is the biggest unknown: Mykonos effectively operates 5-6 months/year (June-September). A 34.4% IRR on a 5-month operating year implies either very strong residence sales or high leverage. The revenue model breakdown is MISSING — this is the most consequential data gap.
  2. 35% ownership — lowest in the portfolio: Who holds the remaining 65%? Landowner? Aman? At 35%, control rights are weak: need tag-along, drag-along, board seats, anti-dilution, information rights clearly defined. Exit could be blocked by the majority.
  3. Greek bureaucracy: "Final amendments to the building permit" can still face months of delay — Greece's administrative system is notoriously slow.
  4. Highest IRR demands highest scrutiny: 34.4% typically accompanies the most aggressive assumptions. Need leverage level + residence sellout schedule reviewed.
  5. Global recession risk (self-flagged) — luxury travel is highly cyclical.
⚠️ Verdict: Most attractive return but requires the most questions answered: seasonality model, who holds 65%, and why the IRR is 14pp higher than Bora Bora. If residence sales assumptions hold, this is the portfolio's return driver.

🔴 Portfolio-Level Critique

  1. NO revenue projections: The Summary Analysis is 100% qualitative. No ADR, occupancy, NOI, or exit cap rates provided → the $486.5M profit claim cannot be DCF-validated. This is the single biggest gap in the entire offering.
  2. NO exit assumptions: No exit year, cap rate, or stabilization NOI. The 2.72x equity multiple is an arithmetic sum, not a cash flow-timed return.
  3. Minority stakes throughout (49/49/35/49%): Zero control over any asset. Governance: board seats, anti-dilution, tag/drag-along, information rights not disclosed. Exit alignment with Aman (majority holder) is a structural risk.
  4. Asset-type concentration: 3/4 are developments (even Bora Bora as a conversion carries construction risk) opening 2028-2030. "Geographic diversification" masks a uniform risk profile: luxury travel cyclicality + construction + residence sales velocity.
  5. Blended IRR ~22-24% is likely pre-fees: Management fees, development fees, acquisition fees, and promote structure not disclosed. PE benchmark is 15-20% net — gross vs net distinction is critical.
  6. Cash call profile: $160M initial (57%) + $122.3M future (43%) — investor must have multi-year liquidity commitments.
  7. FX exposure: EUR (Mykonos, Rosa Alpina), MVR peg (Maldives), XPF (French Polynesia) — no hedging discussion provided.
  8. Execution dependency on Aman: All developments operator-run by Aman. Track record is good (Aman Maldives on budget) but single-operator concentration.
🔴 Overall: The thesis is logical (scarcity, diversification, operating derisk) but the quantitative foundation is entirely absent. Before committing $160M initial, minimum requirements: revenue model per property + sensitivity table (cost +20%, ADR -15%, occ -10pts) + governance term sheet + fee disclosure.
