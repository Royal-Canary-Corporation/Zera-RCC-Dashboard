01 Our Concerns — Explained
⚖️ Concern 1: Advance Payment Risk

What we're seeing: If the contract is terminated early for any reason, 55% of the contract value would already have been paid while only a small portion of the work has been completed.

Why it matters: This is not about trust — it's about standard corporate governance. Our internal policy requires that we have a fair mechanism in place to recover unearned advance payments, similar to how an Advance Payment Bond would function under FIDIC standards. Since bank guarantees from Russian entities are currently unavailable due to sanctions, we need an alternative mechanism.

Our proposed solution: A Clawback Clause — a simple contractual provision that ensures any unearned portion of advance payments is refunded if the contract is terminated. This is a standard, internationally recognized mechanism that protects both parties' interests.

📄 Concern 2: Deliverable Security

What we're seeing: If the project is interrupted, RCC would need to continue with a different PM/CM provider. The cost and time impact of restarting would be significant if design documents, drawings, and project data are not immediately available.

Why it matters: This is standard practice in all international construction contracts — the owner retains full ownership of all deliverables. We simply need this to be clearly stated in the agreement.

Our proposed solution: IP Retention — all deliverables are owned by RCC from the moment of creation. No payment is released until the corresponding deliverables have been physically handed over and accepted. This protects both parties: BEMD gets paid on time, and RCC has what it needs to continue the project.

🛡️ Concern 3: Early Termination

What we're seeing: If BEMD were to decide to terminate the contract without cause during the early stages, RCC would be left with a partially completed project and significant upfront costs.

Why it matters: This is a standard risk management provision — it exists in most professional service contracts worldwide. It's not about expecting termination, but about having a fair framework in place.

Our proposed solution: Graduated Liquidated Damages — a pre-agreed penalty that decreases as the project progresses. This is a genuine pre-estimate of the costs RCC would incur in finding a replacement team. It provides certainty for both parties without requiring lengthy legal proceedings.

02 Proposed Protection Mechanisms

① Clawback Clause

If the contract is terminated, BEMD refunds the portion of advance payments that exceeds the value of services actually performed. Calculation: Clawback = Paid − (% Complete × Contract Value). Capped at 25% of total contract value. Payment within 30 days of determination. Standard FIDIC-aligned mechanism.

② IP Retention + Deliverable Handover

All designs, drawings, BIM models, reports, and project documentation belong to RCC from creation. Each milestone payment is conditional on the physical handover and acceptance of the corresponding deliverables. BEMD retains a license to use the deliverables for this project only.

③ Graduated Liquidated Damages

If BEMD terminates without cause, a pre-agreed penalty applies. The penalty decreases as the project progresses: 5% (early) → 1% (late). This is a genuine pre-estimate of RCC's transition costs, not a punitive measure. Reflects the decreasing risk profile as the project advances.

④ No Bank Guarantee Required

We understand that bank guarantees are not available. The mechanisms above collectively provide the same level of protection without requiring any third-party guarantee. This is designed specifically to accommodate BEMD's current circumstances.
